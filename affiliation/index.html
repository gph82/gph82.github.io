Affiliations#

Current#

The Center for Scalable Data Analytics and Artificial Intelligence, ScaDS.AI, is one of Germany’s five National AI Competence Centers. It is jointly anchored at TU Dresden and Leipzig University, where I am affiliated.

My position is within the Life Science and Medicine research area, and my focus here is the scalable and automated analysis of large-scale data from molecular dynamics and generative AI ensembles. This builds on previous work with ScaDS.AI scientists like Dr. Daniel Wiegreffe and Prof. Peter. W. Hildebrand, which resulted in packages like MDsrv and mdxplain.

In parallel, I am also a guest scientist at the Max Planck Institute for Molecular Genetics in Berlin Dahlem, in the research group of Dr. Dr. Matthew Kraushar: Neurodevelopment and translation control.

There, while quite happily embedded in a highly experimental setting, I computationally study ribosomal proteins and the evolutionary pressures shaping certain aspects of their structural biology.

Previous#

My previous positions at Charité Universitäsmedizin Berlin involved two long-running projects in the Institute of Physiology and the Institute of Medical Physics and Biophysics, working with two different visiting fellows on two distinct subjects:

  • Kinase Modelling - Einstein BIH Visiting Fellow John Chodera:

    Initially hosted at the Institute of Physiology by the In-Silico Toxicology and Structural Bioinformatics lab led by Prof. Andrea Volkamer, which is now the Data Driven Drug Design lab at Saarland University, where I was also employed.

  • GPCR Complexes in Motion - Einstein BIH Visiting Fellow Brian Kobilka:

    Hosted at the Institute for Medical Physics and Biophysics, first at the Hildebrand Lab (now at Universität Leipzig) and then at the Structural Biology of Cellular Signaling (Scheerer Lab). This is where things like mdciao and our collaboration with Georgios Skiniotis on time-resolved cryo-EM microscopy come from.

Before that, I worked at the Department of Mathematics and Computer Science of the Freie Universität Berlin on two consecutive projects:

  • I spent most of my time in the Computational Molecular Biology - AI4Science group of Prof. Frank Noé, now at Microsoft Research.

    My research on GPCRs started in that period, focusing on Statistical Mechanics and Markov State Modeling, tightly coupled with scientific software development, including works like TICA and PyEMMA. These years and this introduction to GPCRs have shaped much of my work since.

  • Before that, I started my first postdoc in the Biocomputing Group of Prof. Christof Schütte under PD Dr. Burkhard Schmidt (now at Weierstrass Institute for Applied Analysis and Stochastics), working for one year on water dynamics inside carbon nanotubes.
